Add a triangulation engine to `trimesh.creation.triangulate_polygon` that is built into the library and is selected with `engine="delaunay"`. Today the function can only triangulate a shapely polygon when one of the optional native packages is installed and raises when none of them are, so this engine has to work with nothing but the required dependencies of the package: it cannot reach for any of the optional triangulation packages, and it cannot reach for scipy either. It is also the engine chosen automatically when none of the optional ones are installed, so a bare install stops failing, while an install that does have one of them keeps resolving to the same engine it resolves to now.

Like the other engines it hands back the vertices and the triangle indexes. The vertices are exactly the vertices of the polygon's rings, each of them once — the repeated point that closes a ring, and any point repeated within a ring, are not separate vertices — and no point is ever invented, so a caller who needs a triangulation over the original vertices always gets one. Every vertex ends up used by at least one triangle, the triangles are wound counter-clockwise, and together they cover the polygon exactly once: nothing extends past the exterior ring, nothing lands inside a hole, and no two triangles overlap. Every segment of the exterior ring and of each interior ring survives as an edge of the result.

Past being merely valid, the result is a constrained Delaunay triangulation, which is what keeps the triangles well shaped instead of leaving the long thin fans that ear clipping tends to produce. Concretely, wherever two triangles share an edge that is not one of those ring segments, neither triangle's circumcircle may strictly contain the opposite vertex of the other.

Rings may arrive wound in either direction and both windings have to give the same triangulation. A vertex sitting in the middle of an otherwise straight edge is an ordinary vertex and has to be used like any other. A polygon that encloses no area yields no triangles.
